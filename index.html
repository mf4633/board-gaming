What is Board Gaming Hub?

Board Gaming Hub is a free collection of browser games built and maintained by one person, Michael Flynn, a licensed civil engineer who works on dams and flood hydrology. Every game is a single HTML file: open the page and play, with no account and no install. Game pages cover the rules and strategy, and the most-played games also explain how that particular version works, including what the computer opponent does and which rule variants were chosen.

The originals are the reason the site exists. Tidelands is a Bronze Age maritime trade game whose scoring was rebalanced after 1,000-game simulation runs. Bisque is a battle for a bay, Agora is Mediterranean trade, Aresia colonizes Mars, Odyssey sails the wine-dark sea, Convergence pits rival civilizations against a shared economy, and Doctrine is a geopolitical simulation running from 1990 to 2050. None of them exist anywhere else.

The classics are here because people play them every day. Solitaire, Sudoku, Mahjong, Minesweeper, Chess and Go sit next to two original daily word puzzles, Wordform and Drift, and four games that are thousands of years old: the Royal Game of Ur, Senet, Pente Grammai and Mancala.

The simulations use real engineering. Floodline and Bonneville Spillway come from the author's day job in flood and dam work, and Eclipse Predictor computes eclipse paths from 1900 to 2200 with the open-source astronomy-engine library.

The site is free and supported by Google ads. Many of the games also have Android versions; see the app list.

Simulations

Real-time physics, engineering, and management sims.

The simulation catalog is what sets this hub apart from a generic game directory. Floodline models California flood defense with levees, pumps, and real storm hydrographs. Bonneville Spillway Operator balances grid demand, salmon passage, and total dissolved gas at a real dam. Eclipse Predictor renders solar eclipse paths on a 3D globe with sub-arcsecond ephemeris accuracy. Tower (SkyStack), Metropolis 2K, Apoapsis, Biosphere Blue, and Doctrine round out management, city-building, orbital mechanics, and geopolitical sims — all built by a practicing engineer who documents the real formulas behind each model.
